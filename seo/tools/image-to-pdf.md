## Order, orientation and page shape

Drag the thumbnails into the order the reader expects. Aadhaar front before back, marksheet page one before page two, and the first page of a contract at the top. Portrait photos on a landscape page waste half the sheet and make a PDF look careless.

Choose A4 when the document will be printed and match-image when it is going back to a portal. Rotating a sideways photo inside the tool is better than forcing the reader to tilt their head.

## Shoot it properly the first time

Put the document on a plain dark surface, use daylight instead of flash, and keep the phone parallel to the page. All four corners inside the frame, no fingers in shot. That single habit reduces the need for cropping and improves the final size.

A 12-megapixel photo of a page is around 3MB. Seven such pages make a 21MB PDF that no portal accepts, so convert and compress in the same sitting instead of trying to fix it after submission.

## Where the file goes

Nothing is uploaded. The images are read locally, written into a PDF in your browser and downloaded to your device. For identity documents and bank statements this is the only sensible default.

If the final PDF needs to be smaller still, run it through the PDF compressor with a target size before you upload it.

## FAQs (FAQPage schema ready)

### How many images can I combine into one PDF?

There is no fixed server limit because conversion is local. Very large sets depend on your device memory; splitting into batches of 20–30 pages is comfortable on phones.

### Will the PDF text be searchable?

No. Images inside a PDF are pictures until you run OCR. If you need selectable text, use an OCR step after conversion.

### Can I mix JPG and PNG in the same PDF?

Yes. Portable Network Graphics with transparency are flattened onto a white page, which is what you want for printing.

### Are my documents uploaded to a server?

No. Everything is generated on your device, so ID photos and statements never leave your browser.
