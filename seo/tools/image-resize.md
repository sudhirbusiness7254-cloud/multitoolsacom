## Width, height and the aspect-ratio lock

Type the width and the height follows if the lock icon is on, which prevents the stretched-face problem. Turn the lock off only when a form demands a fixed shape, such as a 600 x 600 square or a 3.5 x 4.5 cm portrait.

Resizing downward keeps quality well. Resizing upward invents pixels, so a 400px photo blown up to 1200px will look soft no matter which tool does it. Always start from the largest version you have.

## Sizes that actually get accepted

These are the numbers people search for most often. Always re-check the specific form because requirements change between recruitment cycles.

- Passport photo 3.5 x 4.5 cm: about 200 x 230 px at 150 DPI, 20–50KB JPG
- PAN card photo: 213 x 213 px square, signature 400 x 200 px
- Aadhaar update photo: 200 x 230 px, under 50KB
- Instagram feed portrait: 1080 x 1350 px; square: 1080 x 1080 px
- YouTube thumbnail: 1280 x 720 px, under 2MB
- LinkedIn banner: 1584 x 396 px; profile photo: 400 x 400 px

## Resizing is not compressing

A 5000 x 4000 photo shrunk to 600 x 480 does become a smaller file, but the quality setting still decides the bytes. If a portal says 20KB, resize to the required pixels and then compress with the target-size box. Doing only one of the two is why uploads bounce.

For documents, keep the long edge at 1000–1600 px. Below that, small print in a marksheet or a bank statement becomes unreadable when an officer zooms in.

## FAQs (FAQPage schema ready)

### Will resizing reduce the file size?

Usually yes, because fewer pixels means less data, but the compression quality still matters. For a hard limit like 20KB, resize to the required dimensions and then use the compressor's target-size option.

### How do I resize without stretching the photo?

Keep the aspect-ratio lock on and change only one side. If the required shape is different from your photo, crop to that shape first instead of squeezing the image.

### What DPI should I use for a printed photo?

300 DPI for anything printed at photo quality, 150 DPI for forms where the photo is only scanned or checked. DPI only matters together with physical size in centimetres.

### Can I resize to a percentage instead of pixels?

Yes. Switch the unit selector to percent, which is handy when making thumbnails or half-size copies for email.
